Caught Clean style guide

The tokens, layout and content rules behind the "Caught Clean" carousel deck, so the next one matches without re-deriving the choices. Built from the first three-slide deck (the failure, the stake, the fix).

Platform rules

LinkedIn's own carousel spec. Every rule below is a hard constraint, checked before a deck ships — not a style choice.

  • Export as PDF. LinkedIn's carousel format is a multi-page PDF, not a set of separate images.
  • Under 100 MB, 2–300 pages. 5–15 pages performs best; this deck's 3 pages sits below that band, which is a deliberate trade for a tight pyramid rather than an oversight.
  • 1:1 (1080 × 1080px) or 4:5 (1080 × 1350px). This system uses 4:5 throughout, so a slide fills more of the feed before a reader scrolls past.
  • Source each page as JPEG, PNG or PDF before assembling the final multi-page PDF.
  • Keep the bottom-right corner clear. LinkedIn overlays its page counter and zoom control there on every page. No wash, headline, caption or page marker may sit in that corner — see the layout diagram below.

Layout

4:5 card (1080 × 1350px for export). One wash, anchored to a corner other than bottom-right, never centered behind the type.

kicker
headline
caption + foot
keep clear
  1. Margin34px sides, 34px top and bottom on a 1080px card. Never let the wash bleed under the type — keep 60px of clear paper around every line.
  2. Vertical orderKicker, then headline, then a flexible gap, then caption and foot pinned to the bottom edge.
  3. One wash per slidePlaced in a single corner other than bottom-right, at most 55% of the card's width. It reads as a wash, not a background.
  4. Bottom-right stays empty. Reserve roughly the bottom-right 20% × 14% of the card for LinkedIn's own page counter and zoom control. Put the page marker bottom-left instead.
  5. Sketch markAt most once per deck, saved for the resolution slide. A single hand-drawn stroke (a check, an underline, an arrow), never decorative repetition, and never inside the bottom-right corner.

Watercolor washes

Drawn on canvas as one soft, wobbled blob per wash, not a CSS gradient hero. Two washes rotate through a deck; a third slide can combine both, faded, to close the sequence.

Slide 1 — sage, lower left. 50% opacity, blur radius ~35% of blob size. Lower left, not lower right, to clear LinkedIn's page-counter corner.
Slide 2 — blush, upper left. Opposite corner from slide 1, same opacity band.
Slide 3 — both, faded further. Signals closure without adding a third color.

Background variants

Same wash technique, three compositions. Pick one per deck, not per slide — a deck stays on one composition throughout so the set reads as a family.

Corner (default). One wobbled blob anchored to a single corner. Reads as a signature, works for most topics.
Horizon. A wide, flattened wash low across the card, like a wash line on paper. Calmer, suits a slower or more reflective topic.
Scatter. Two small blobs near the top corners, away from the type block entirely. Airier; suits a lighter or more upbeat topic.

Color variants

Four palettes, same corner composition, so only the color changes. Each still follows the palette rule above: warm paper ground, near-black headline ink, two low-saturation washes, one accent doing kicker and sketch-mark duty.

Sage & blush (default). #94B2A6 / #E3B2A6, accent #8A5A44. Neutral; the deck built so far.
Slate & ochre. #9FB0C2 / #E3C48A, accent #3E5C76. Cooler and more analytical; fits a data-led or technical topic.
Moss & clay. #A8B58C / #D19C7C, accent #5B6B45. Earthier; fits a process or craft topic.
Lilac & sand. #B7A8C9 / #E4CFAE, accent #6B4E71. Softer; fits a people or culture topic.

Pick one palette per deck for the whole three-slide set. Don't mix palettes within a deck, and don't introduce a fifth — add a new named palette here first if a topic genuinely needs one.

Content formula

Each deck maps one article's pyramid onto three slides. The formula, not the specific words, is what repeats.

  • Slide 1, the hook: the article's opening fact, stated flat, no adjective doing the work. Kicker names the pyramid stage ("The failure", "The finding", "The gap").
  • Slide 2, the reader's stake: who bears the cost and what it costs them, drawn from the article's "why this reader cares" paragraph. Never the mechanism twice; that's slide 1's job.
  • Slide 3, the resolution: the fix or the number that proves it, plus one line the reader can act on. This is the only slide that earns the sketch mark and the combined wash.
  • Word budget per slide: headline under 8 words, caption under 14. If either forces a third color or a second wash, cut the copy, don't add the slide.
  • Page count: LinkedIn's own data favors 5–15 pages. A three-beat pyramid stays at 3 slides on purpose; a source article with more distinct findings can extend to one slide per finding, up to that 15-page band, before adding a fourth beat.
  • No invented specifics. Every number or quote on a slide must trace to the source article. A style guide governs how it looks, not what it's allowed to claim.
